Best 30-min windows
This dashboard pulls live data from three free UK public APIs every 30 minutes and transforms it through a medallion data pipeline (Bronze to Silver to Gold). The charts below reflect real grid conditions, not forecasts.

Generation Fuel Types

Gas Fossil
The UK's single largest power source. Burns natural gas in a combined cycle (gas turbine + steam turbine), reaching ~58% efficiency. Fires up quickly to meet demand peaks. Labelled CCGT in grid data.
Wind Renewable
Combined onshore and offshore wind. The UK has the world's largest offshore wind capacity. Output varies with weather: on a windy day wind can supply over 60% of UK electricity.
Nuclear Low carbon
Fission-based baseload power. The UK has 5 operational plants (as of 2025). Runs continuously at near-constant output; it doesn't ramp up or down easily. Produces near-zero operational carbon. Hinkley Point C is under construction.
Biomass Renewable*
Primarily Drax power station burning compressed wood pellets. Classified as renewable under UK policy (carbon considered re-absorbed by new forest growth), though this is contested. Provides flexible, schedulable generation unlike wind/solar.
Hydroelectric Renewable
Run-of-river and reservoir hydro generating from natural water flow. Concentrated in Scotland and Wales. Provides clean, reliable baseload with low variability. Distinct from pumped storage, which stores energy rather than generating it.
Pumped Storage Storage
Water pumped uphill using cheap overnight electricity, then released through turbines during demand peaks: a giant water battery. Dinorwig in Wales ("Electric Mountain") can go from zero to 1.7 GW in 16 seconds.
Gas (peaker) Fossil / Peaker
Open-cycle gas turbines, less efficient than standard gas (~35%) but start in minutes. Used only during the highest demand periods. Higher carbon per MWh. You'll see these appear during grid stress events on cold, calm evenings.
Coal Fossil
The UK's last coal plant (Ratcliffe-on-Soar) closed in September 2024, so this should be at or near zero. Any non-zero reading is likely a small reserve or data artefact. The UK was the birthplace of coal power.
Oil Fossil
Oil-fired generation. Essentially zero in normal operation, kept for extreme emergencies only. A non-trivial reading here means something unusual is happening on the grid.
Other Mixed
Catch-all for smaller generation sources not individually categorised: landfill gas, sewage gas, energy-from-waste plants, and some smaller embedded generators not tracked in real time by BMRS.

Interconnectors

The UK is connected to continental Europe and Ireland via high-voltage DC cables under the sea. Positive values = UK importing; negative = UK exporting. They balance the grid across borders: when UK wind is low, France's nuclear output fills the gap.

INTELEC / INTEW
England–Wales interconnectors to France (IFA) and other legacy links. IFA has 2 GW capacity.
INTFR
IFA2, second France interconnector (1 GW), opened 2021. Routes via Fareham to Normandy.
INTIRL
Moyle interconnector to Northern Ireland (500 MW). Cross-border balancing within the island of Ireland Single Electricity Market.
INTGRNL
NEMO Link to Belgium (1 GW, 140 km), operational since 2019. Connects National Grid to Elia.
INTNED
BritNed, UK to Netherlands (1 GW). Connects Isle of Grain to Maasvlakte near Rotterdam.
INTNEM
North Sea Link, UK to Norway (1.4 GW, 720 km), the world's longest subsea cable. Opened 2021. Trades UK wind for Norwegian hydro.
INTIFA2
IFA2 capacity allocation. May appear separately from INTFR in BMRS data depending on settlement period.
INTVKL
Viking Link, UK to Denmark (1.4 GW, 765 km), the world's longest land and subsea cable. Opened December 2023.

Dashboard Metrics Explained

Settlement Period
The UK electricity market operates in 30-minute "settlement periods" (48 per day). All generation, consumption and pricing is settled at this granularity. Period 1 = 00:00–00:30 UTC.
Carbon Intensity (gCO₂/kWh)
Grams of CO₂ equivalent emitted per kilowatt-hour of electricity consumed. Includes the full generation mix. Under 100 is very clean; over 300 is dirty (heavy gas use). The UK average has fallen from ~500 in 2012 to under 150 today.
Renewable %
Fraction of total generation from wind, hydroelectric, and pumped storage. Nuclear is low-carbon but not renewable. Biomass is renewable under UK policy but excluded here to be conservative.
7-Day Rolling Average
The average renewable % computed over the previous 168 hours (7 × 24). Smooths out daily and weather-driven variation so you can see the underlying trend.
Grid Stress Hour
An hour flagged as high-risk for carbon spikes: temperature below 7°C AND windspeed below 20 km/h AND time between 16:00–20:00 AND renewable % below 30%. These are the conditions when gas peakers fire and carbon intensity surges. Typically December–February.
Temp vs Demand slope
The regression coefficient from the scatter chart. A typical value is −500 to −800 MW/°C on weekdays, meaning each 1°C drop in temperature adds roughly half a gigawatt to national demand, mainly from heating.
Demand Profile
Average grid demand by hour of day, split weekday vs weekend. The weekday "morning ramp" (~06:00–09:00) and "evening peak" (~17:00–19:00) are driven by commuting patterns. Weekend profiles are flatter and shifted later.
Carbon Heatmap
Best hours to run high-electricity appliances (EV charging, washing machine, dishwasher). Green cells = high renewable share, low carbon. Weekend mornings are typically cleanest; winter weekday evenings are dirtiest.
BMRS
Balancing Mechanism Reporting Service, operated by Elexon on behalf of National Grid. The official source of UK half-hourly generation data. All licensed generators above 50 MW must report here.

Data Pipeline

Raw API responses land in Bronze tables with no transformation. DBT cleans and types the data into Silver staging models (UTC timestamps, deduplication, region lookups). Gold mart tables join the three sources and compute aggregates: these are what the charts query directly. The pipeline runs every 30 minutes via cron.
